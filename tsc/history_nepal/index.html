    इतिहास

    The Malla period (c. 1258–1826 B.S.) is considered the Golden Age of Newari art, culture, and architecture in the Kathmandu Valley. Below is a comprehensive timeline of the prominent Malla kings and their major contributions, with all dates provided in Bikram Sambat (B.S.).

    1. Early Malla Period (United Kingdom)

    Before the valley was divided, several powerful monarchs ruled from a single center (primarily Bhaktapur).

    • Ari Malla (c. 1258–1273 B.S.): The first king to adopt the title "Malla." Legend says he was wrestling (Malla-yuddha) when he heard of his son's birth and chose the title to signify strength.

    • Abhaya Malla (c. 1273–1312 B.S.): His reign was marked by a devastating earthquake in 1312 B.S., which killed nearly one-third of the population, including the King himself.

    • Jayasthiti Malla (c. 1437–1452 B.S.): One of the most influential Malla kings.

    • Social Reform: Codified the caste system based on professions.

    • Economic Reform: Standardized measurements like Mana and Pathi.

    • Culture: A great patron of Sanskrit and Newari literature.

    • Yaksha Malla (c. 1485–1539 B.S.): The last king of the united Kathmandu Valley.

    • Works: Built the Dattatreya Temple and the Pashupati Temple in Bhaktapur.

    • Legacy: After his death in 1539 B.S., the kingdom was divided among his sons, leading to the creation of the three independent city-states of Kantipur, Patan, and Bhaktapur.

    2. Divided Malla Kingdoms (Late Malla Period)

    After the division, each city-state competed to build the most magnificent temples and palaces.

    A. Kantipur (Kathmandu)

    • Mahendra Malla (c. 1617–1631 B.S.): Introduced the first silver coins, known as Mahendra Malli.

    • Laxmi Narasimha Malla (c. 1677–1698 B.S.): Credited with building the Kasthamandap (the Wooden Pavilion) from the wood of a single Sal tree.

    • Pratap Malla (c. 1698–1731 B.S.): The most famous king of Kantipur.

    • Works: Built Rani Pokhari, Hanuman Dhoka Palace, and installed the Kal Bhairab statue.

    • Culture: He was a polyglot and poet, claiming the title "Kavindra" (King of Poets).

    • Jaya Prakash Malla (c. 1792–1825 B.S.): The last king of Kathmandu.

    • Works: Started the Kumari Jatra and Indra Jatra chariot festivals.

    B. Lalitpur (Patan) [10]

    __Siddhi Narasimha Malla (c. 1675–1717 B.S.): A deeply religious king. * Works: Built the world-famous Krishna Mandir in Patan Durbar Square.

    Shree Niwas Malla (c. 1717–1741 B.S.): Reconstructed the Bhimsen Temple and the Machhendranath Temple.

    __Yoga Narendra Malla (c. 1741–1762 B.S.): Known for the legend of the golden bird on his statue. He started the Bhimsen Jatra in Patan.

    C. Bhaktapur (Bhadgaon)

    • Jagat Jyoti Malla (c. 1670–1694 B.S.): Introduced the Bisket Jatra festival and was a renowned playwright.

    • Jitamitra Malla (c. 1730–1753 B.S.): Built the Shikhara-style Shiva temple and founded the National Art Gallery.

    • Bhupatindra Malla (c. 1753–1779 B.S.): The most celebrated builder of Bhaktapur.

    • Works: Built the five-story Nyatapola Temple and the 55-Window Palace.

    • Ranajit Malla (c. 1779–1826 B.S.): The last Malla king of Bhaktapur. He completed the Golden Gate (Lu Dhowka).

    3. End of the Malla Era

    The Malla dynasty came to an end during the unification campaign led by Prithvi Narayan Shah.

    • 1825 B.S.: Kathmandu fell to Gorkhali forces during the Indra Jatra festival.
    • 1825 B.S.: Patan was conquered shortly after.
    • 1826 B.S.: Bhaktapur was the last to fall, marking the official end of Malla rule in the Kathmandu Valley.

    The Shah Time

    The Shah dynasty shifted the focus from the cultural refinement of the Kathmandu Valley to the military unification of modern Nepal. Here is the timeline of the Shah kings from the beginning of the unification to the start of the Rana regime in 1903 B.S.

    1. The Great Unifier

    • Prithvi Narayan Shah (1779–1831 B.S.)

    • 1825 B.S.: Conquered Kantipur (Kathmandu) during the Indra Jatra festival.

    • 1825 B.S.: Conquered Lalitpur (Patan).
    • 1826 B.S.: Conquered Bhaktapur, ending Malla rule.
    • 1826 B.S.: Officially declared Kathmandu the capital of Great Nepal.
    • Works: Wrote the Divya Upadesh, a collection of teachings on governance and foreign policy ("A yam between two boulders").
    • Death: Passed away at Devighat, Nuwakot, in 1831 B.S.

    2. Expansion and Regency Period

    Following Prithvi Narayan Shah, a series of minor kings led to a period of "Regency" where Queens and Chautariyas (relatives) held power.

    • Pratap Singh Shah (1831–1834 B.S.)

    • The eldest son of Prithvi Narayan Shah. His short reign saw the annexation of Chitwan.

    • Rana Bahadur Shah (1834–1855 B.S.)

    • Ascended the throne as an infant.

    • Expansion: Under the regency of Queen Rajendra Laxmi and later Bahadur Shah (the King's uncle), the unification reached its peak, extending from the Tista River in the east to the Mahakali River in the west (1844–1847 B.S.).
    • 1855 B.S.: Abdicated in favor of his infant son, Girvan Yuddha, and became a Sanyasi (Swami Nirgunanda).

    3. The Anglo-Nepal War Era

    • Girvan Yuddha Bikram Shah (1855–1873 B.S.)

    • 1863 B.S.: Bhimsen Thapa became the Mukhtiyar (Prime Minister) after the assassination of Rana Bahadur Shah.

    • 1871–1873 B.S.: The Anglo-Nepal War occurred.
    • 1872 B.S. (Mangsir): The Sugauli Treaty was signed, forcing Nepal to cede about one-third of its territory (including Sikkim, Kumaon, and Garhwal) to the British East India Company.

    4. Internal Conflict and the Rise of Jung Bahadur

    • Rajendra Bikram Shah (1873–1904 B.S.)

    • His reign was marked by intense court conspiracies between the Thapa, Pande, and Rana factions.

    • 1894 B.S.: Fall of Bhimsen Thapa, leading to his eventual suicide in prison.
    • 1903 B.S. (Asar): General Gagan Singh, a favorite of Queen Rajya Laxmi, was mysteriously assassinated.

    5. The End of Shah Authority (Start of Rana Regime)

    • The Kot Massacre (1903 B.S., Ashwin 2):\ To find Gagan Singh's killer, Queen Rajya Laxmi summoned all officials to the Kot courtyard. A fight broke out, and Jung Bahadur Kunwar (later Rana) executed a bloody coup, killing nearly 40 top nobles and officials.
    • The Bhandarkhal Massacre (1903 B.S., Kartik):\ The Queen tried to assassinate Jung Bahadur, but he discovered the plot, killed his rivals, and exiled the King and Queen to Varanasi.

    1903 B.S. marks the year Jung Bahadur Rana became the Prime Minister, stripping the Shah kings of their executive powers and making the role of Prime Minister hereditary, beginning 104 years of Rana rule.

    The Rana Regime Begins

    The Rana regime, established in 1903 B.S. following the Kot Massacre, lasted for 104 years. During this period, the Shah Kings remained as figurehead monarchs while the Rana Prime Ministers held absolute executive power as the Shree 3 Maharaja.

    Timeline of Rana Prime Ministers and Major Works

    Prime Minister Period (B.S.) Major Contributions & Events
    Jung Bahadur Rana 1903–1933 First Rana PM; introduced the first national legal code, Muluki Ain (1910 B.S.); established Durbar High School; visited Europe in 1906 B.S.
    Ranodip Singh Rana 1933–1942 Published the first official calendar (Patro); established a system for land revenue collection; assassinated by his Shumsher nephews.
    Bir Shumsher JBR 1942–1957 Established Bir Hospital (1947 B.S.), Bir Library, and Ghanta Ghar; introduced Bir Dhara (piped water system) in Kathmandu.
    Dev Shumsher JBR 1957–1958 Progressive ruler; started the Gorkhapatra newspaper; fixed government office hours (10 am to 5 pm); was deposed after just 114 days.
    Chandra Shumsher JBR 1958–1986 Abolished the Sati Pratha (1977 B.S.) and slavery (1981 B.S.); established Tri-Chandra College (1975 B.S.); introduced electricity to Nepal.
    Bhim Shumsher JBR 1986–1989 Abolished customs duties on salt and cotton; encouraged cotton cultivation.
    Juddha Shumsher JBR 1989–2002 Established Nepal Bank (1994 B.S.); built the fire brigade, zoo, and museum; led reconstruction after the 1990 B.S. Great Earthquake.
    Padma Shumsher JBR 2002–2005 Promulgated the first constitution of Nepal (Government of Nepal Act, 2004 B.S.); known as a "Liberal Rana."
    Mohan Shumsher JBR 2005–2008 Last Rana PM; signed the 1950 (2007 B.S.) Treaty with India; his reign ended with the Revolution of 2007 B.S. which restored the King's power.

    Key Historical Transitions

    • Succession Shift (1942 B.S.): Power shifted from the direct line of Jung Bahadur to the Shumsher family (sons of Dhir Shumsher) after the assassination of Ranodip Singh.
    • The End of Autocracy (2007 B.S.): Following an armed revolution led by the Nepali Congress and King Tribhuvan's flight to India, the Delhi Compromise ended the 104-year-old hereditary rule on Falgun 7, 2007 B.S. [7, 8, 9, 10]

    The unification of Nepal, led by King Prithvi Narayan Shah, was a strategic military campaign that transformed the small Gorkha Kingdom into a unified nation.

    Here is the detailed timeline of the unification period (all dates in Bikram Sambat):

    1. The Early Struggles (Beginning)

    • 1794 B.S.: Prithvi Narayan Shah's first attempt to conquer Nuwakot (the gateway to the Kathmandu Valley) as a Crown Prince. He failed due to a lack of preparation and modern weaponry.
    • 1799 B.S.: He ascended the throne of Gorkha after the death of his father, Narabhupal Shah.
    • 1801 B.S. (Ashwin 15): Conquest of Nuwakot. After training his troops and procuring better arms from Varanasi, he successfully captured Nuwakot, securing a strategic base near the Valley.

    2. Expanding the Perimeter

    • 1814 B.S.: The First Battle of Kirtipur. The Gorkhali army suffered a heavy defeat. Kalu Pande, the commander-in-chief, was killed, and the King barely escaped.
    • 1819 B.S.: Conquest of Makawanpur. This was crucial as it controlled the trade routes to India. The Gorkhalis defeated King Digbandhan Sen.
    • 1819 B.S. (Magh): Battle against Gurgin Khan. The Nawab of Bengal sent an army under Gurgin Khan to help the King of Makawanpur. The Gorkhalis defeated them, capturing 500 guns and cannons which strengthened their military.
    • 1821 B.S.: The Second Battle of Kirtipur. Another failure for Gorkha; the King's brother, Surpratap Shah, lost an eye in this battle.

    3. The Fall of the Valley

    • 1822 B.S. (Chaitra): Conquest of Kirtipur. On the third attempt, the Gorkhalis captured the hill-town through a blockade and psychological warfare.
    • 1824 B.S. (Ashwin): Battle of Sindhuli Gadhi. The British East India Company sent Captain Kinloch with 2,400 soldiers to assist the Malla kings. The Gorkhalis used guerrilla tactics in the dense jungles to defeat the British forces.
    • 1825 B.S. (Ashwin 13): Conquest of Kantipur (Kathmandu). The Gorkhalis entered the city during the Indra Jatra festival while the locals were celebrating. King Jaya Prakash Malla fled to Patan.
    • 1825 B.S. (Ashwin 20): Conquest of Lalitpur (Patan). The city surrendered without much resistance after the fall of Kathmandu.
    • 1826 B.S. (Kartik 25): Conquest of Bhaktapur. The three Malla kings (of Kathmandu, Patan, and Bhaktapur) had gathered here. After a brief but bloody battle, the Malla era officially ended.

    4. Post-Valley Expansion (The "Great Nepal" Phase)

    • 1830 B.S.: Conquest of Majhkirat (the region between the Dudh Koshi and Arun rivers).
    • 1831 B.S.: Conquest of Pallokirat (the Limbuwan region near the eastern border).
    • 1831 B.S. (Magh 1): Death of Prithvi Narayan Shah. He died at Devighat, Nuwakot, leaving the unification incomplete.

    5. Completion of the Map (Regency Period)

    • 1834–1842 B.S.: Under the leadership of Queen Rajendra Laxmi (Regent), the Gorkhali army conquered several Chaubisi states (including Lamjung, Tanahun, and Kaski).

    • 1843–1849 B.S.: Under the leadership of Bahadur Shah (the youngest son of P.N. Shah), the campaign pushed west.

    • 1846 B.S.: Conquest of the Baise states (western Nepal).

    • 1847 B.S.: Conquest of Kumaon (present-day India).

    • 1848 B.S.: Conquest of Garhwal (present-day India).

    • 1861 B.S.: The Gorkhali army reached the Sutlej River, marking the furthest western extent of Nepal.

    The unification campaign effectively paused with the start of the Anglo-Nepal War in 1871 B.S.
